Chapter 01

The Crowded Mind

Mara had a thought for every silence.

Scroll to begin

Mara had a thought for every silence.

By morning, her notebook was full, and she was still afraid to begin.

She trusted the first thought for exactly three seconds.

Then doubt arrived with a red pen.

Every possibility walked beside her. None of them knew where they were going.

She ordered tea, then wondered if coffee would have made the decision easier.

A small thought became a question. The question became a weather system.

Behind every door, she imagined another door. So she stayed in the hallway.

MAYBE, WHAT IF, TOMORROW, SOMEDAY, A DIFFERENT LIFE, ANOTHER WAY, START OVER, IF ONLY, BEGIN AGAIN, SOMETHING BETTER, WAIT LONGER, NOT YET, NOT HERE, NOT NOW, TURN BACK, MAYBE NOT

Chapter 02

A Bird & A Bookseller

Write something that cannot be improved.

Scroll to begin

The bird arrived without an explanation. Mara envied its confidence.

The pages moved in the wind, making a language out of unfinished things.

“
Write something that cannot be improved,
the bookseller said.

Then see what survives.
”

The blank page was not empty. It was simply refusing to choose for her.

Mara wrote:

“I am here.”

It was not original. It was true.

Ink to Ash

The ink curled into smoke.

Scroll to begin

She revised the truth until it almost disappeared.

For once, she did not solve the feeling. She let the rain name it.

The words ran together. Their edges softened. Nothing ended.

She had mistaken preservation for care. Some things needed to be released.

The ink curled into smoke. Her thoughts became lighter without becoming less real.

What remained was not an answer.

It was room.

Chapter 04

Begin Again

She left the first sentence where it landed.

Scroll to begin

She opened the window before she knew why. The morning entered anyway.

The bird left no instructions. Mara was beginning to understand the gift.

Without a page to defend, her hands felt unfamiliar.

She chose left.

Not because it was right, but because it was a direction.

The child did not ask what the boat meant. Mara admired that.

They made another one. The fold was crooked. It floated.

The room had not changed. Her relationship with it had.

Mara began again.

This time, she left the first sentence where it landed.
